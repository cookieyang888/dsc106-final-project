READING THE DATA

One model.
A window into possibilities.

These results come from CMIP6 CESM2 / NCAR, member r4i1p1f1, native grid gn, monthly near-surface air temperature (tas). Locations refer to the nearest coarse model grid cell, not observed city temperatures. Scenario labels identify alternative pathways; they are not probabilities.

All changes use 1985–2014 as the baseline, not a preindustrial baseline. Annual means are weighted by days in the model’s no-leap calendar. The trend view uses trailing 20-year means, labeled by the last year in each window; the first complete scenario-only window ends in 2034. Faint lines preserve annual variability.

The seasonal view averages each calendar month across your selected years and subtracts that month’s historical average. The comparison table averages annual anomalies over the same selected period. A single model member does not represent the range of model uncertainty, and these local anomalies should not be read as global warming targets.

INITIAL PROTOTYPE · NOVEMBER 18, 2026

Where the project stands.

What have we done so far?

We prepared monthly and annual temperature projections for London, New York, San Diego, and New Delhi under three emissions pathways. We built an interactive globe as the opening view of the project. Readers can drag to rotate the globe, zoom in or out, and select a city to fly to it. Rotating a city near the center updates the introduction and its projected warming. Below the globe, we built a monthly comparison, a trend chart, a seasonal chart, and a table that compares the four cities.

What will be most challenging to design, and why?

Our biggest design challenge will be extending the interactive approach of the opening throughout the project. We want readers’ choices to guide when and how charts and explanations appear. The transitions need to help readers understand why each new view matters and how it connects to what they just explored. We also need to give readers room to explore while keeping the main story easy to follow.

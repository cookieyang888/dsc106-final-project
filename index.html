    READING THE DATA

    One model.
    A window into possibilities.

    These results come from CMIP6 CESM2 / NCAR, member r4i1p1f1, native grid gn, monthly near-surface air temperature (tas). Locations refer to the nearest coarse model grid cell, not observed city temperatures. Scenario labels identify alternative pathways; they are not probabilities.

    All changes use 1985–2014 as the baseline, not a preindustrial baseline. Annual means are weighted by days in the model’s no-leap calendar. The trend view uses trailing 20-year means, labeled by the last year in each window; the first complete scenario-only window ends in 2034. Faint lines preserve annual variability.

    The seasonal view averages each calendar month across your selected years and subtracts that month’s historical average. The comparison table averages annual anomalies over the same selected period. A single model member does not represent the range of model uncertainty, and these local anomalies should not be read as global warming targets.

    INITIAL PROTOTYPE · NOVEMBER 18, 2026

    Where the project stands.

    What have we done so far?

    We have prepared monthly and annual temperature data for four locations and three future pathways from the CESM2 model. We have built a D3 trend chart that switches between annual values and 20-year trailing averages. Brushing the chart or entering years updates a linked seasonal view and a location-comparison table. Location and pathway controls let readers compare patterns, and tooltips expose exact values. We have also built a guided comparison that matches cities by annual warming and reveals where their monthly changes differ. Our methods section documents the baseline, calculations, and limits of a single simulation.

    What will be most challenging to design, and why?

    The most challenging task will be turning these linked views into a clear explanation without overwhelming the reader with controls. Annual variability can obscure scenario differences, so the design must explain what smoothing reveals and what it hides. Seasonal and annual averages also use different calculations, making consistent labels and annotations essential. We need to refine the balance between guided storytelling and open exploration so readers can reach a meaningful takeaway. We will use staff feedback and usability testing to improve the annotations, mobile layout, and clarity of the interactions.
